 Township Infrastructure Management System
 (TIMS)
 Complete Actor Roles, Functionalities, Modules & End-to-End Workflow
Project Scope
TIMS is a township-level infrastructure management platform that manages the complete lifecycle of infrastructure
issues: reporting, validation, triage, contractor estimation, AMC-based approval, execution, billing, payment
authorization, and resident verification.
The system uses Annual Maintenance Contracts (AMCs) with predefined contractor rates. TIMS therefore manages
contractual rates, work quantities, approvals and billing rather than township-owned inventory.

2. RWA Representative
The RWA Representative is the external representative connecting residents with township infrastructure operations.
Report an Issue
  Create a complaint with category, description, location, severity and photographs.
  Select the relevant infrastructure domain such as Civil, Electrical or Water.
 
Track Complaints
  View the current ticket status, assigned contractor, expected completion and progress.
 
View Resolution Evidence
  View before/after photographs and completion information submitted by the contractor.
 
Verify Completed Work
  Confirm that the issue has been resolved.
  Dispute or reopen the ticket if the work is incomplete or unsatisfactory.
  A defined auto-close period can be used when the RWA does not respond.
 
3. Field Contractor

The Field Contractor performs technical inspection and ground-level execution under the applicable AMC.
Receive Work Orders
  View assigned work orders and their priority/SLA information.
 
Inspect the Site
  Visit the location and determine the actual problem and required work.
  Record required labour, work quantities and applicable contractual items.
 
Prepare AMC-Based Estimate
  Select predefined contractual rate items from the applicable AMC.
  Enter quantities or labour hours; TIMS calculates the estimate using the contractual rate.
  The contractor does not arbitrarily enter a new rate for an AMC-covered item.
 
Execute Work
  Update the work through Assigned → In Progress → Completed.
  Record actual work/quantities and upload mandatory before/after evidence.
 
Handle Reopened Work
  Respond to RWA disputes and perform corrective work when a completed ticket is reopened.
 
4. Desk Clerk
Desk Clerks are operational users responsible for departmental triage and work-order coordination. Separate clerks
may operate for Civil, Electrical and Water.
Complaint Validation
  Review incoming complaints, photographs, location and selected category.
  Check whether the complaint belongs to the correct department.
 
Duplicate Detection
  Use location-based duplicate detection to associate nearby reports with an existing master ticket when appropriate.
 
Work Order Creation
  Convert a validated complaint into a work order.
  Assign the work to an appropriate empanelled AMC contractor.
 
Queue Monitoring
  Monitor pending, assigned, in-progress, completed and SLA-at-risk work.
 
Escalation
  Escalate SLA breaches, technical exceptions, contractor delays and other issues to the Department Head.
 
5. Department Head
The Department Head manages one infrastructure department and acts as the main managerial approval/escalation
layer.

6. Finance Clerk
The Finance Clerk performs financial verification after work is completed.
Estimate and Invoice Verification
  Compare the approved estimate, actual work/quantity and invoice.
 
AMC Rate Verification
  Check that billed rates correspond to the applicable AMC/rate card.
 
Variance Handling
  Flag differences between expected and actual quantities or costs for investigation.
 
Payment Authorization
  Once financial checks pass, authorize the invoice for payment processing.
 
7. Township COO
The Township COO is the top operational management role and focuses on township-wide performance rather than
individual routine tickets.
Township Dashboard
  View total complaints, open work, SLA compliance, resolution times, departmental performance and expenditure.
 
Major Financial Approval
  Approve major expenditure requests that require COO-level authorization.
 
Performance Monitoring
  Review department and contractor performance using aggregated operational data.
 
Strategic Oversight
  Monitor township-wide trends in infrastructure issues, spending and service performance.

9. Township & Location Hierarchy
Township → Sector/Zone → Block/Street → Infrastructure Asset → Complaint → Work Order
  The township is the top-level operational boundary.
  Sectors/zones and blocks provide more precise geographic organization.
  Infrastructure assets can be associated with a location and department.
  Complaints can be linked to the relevant asset/location.
 
10. Infrastructure / Asset Management
TIMS should maintain a basic record of township infrastructure so that complaints are connected to known assets
rather than existing only as isolated tickets.
  Civil: roads, drains, footpaths, manholes and public structures.
  Electrical: street lights, transformers, electrical panels and related infrastructure.
  Water: pipelines, pumps, valves, tanks and leakage-related infrastructure.
  Asset information can include Asset ID, type, location, department, condition, installation information and
 maintenance history.

contract with the township.
  Contractor name and contract number.

  Rate card containing labour, service and work-item rates.
  Applicable terms and conditions.

12. Complete End-to-End Workflow
1. Reporting
RWA Representative submits a complaint with category, description, location, severity and photographs.
2. Location & Duplicate Check
TIMS validates the location and checks whether a nearby existing issue should be associated with the new report.
3. Desk Triage
The relevant departmental Desk Clerk validates the complaint and determines whether a work order should be
created.
4. Work Order Creation
A validated issue becomes a work order and is assigned to an appropriate AMC contractor.
5. Contractor Inspection
The contractor visits the site, inspects the issue and records the required work/quantities.
6. AMC-Based Estimation
The contractor selects applicable contractual rate items. TIMS calculates cost using AMC rates and entered
quantities/hours.
7. Financial Approval
The system evaluates the estimate against configured approval thresholds and routes it to the appropriate authority.
8. Work Execution
After approval, the contractor moves the work to In Progress, performs the repair and records actual work/quantities.
9. Completion Evidence
The contractor uploads after-work evidence and marks the work Completed.
10. Finance Verification
Finance compares approved estimate, actual quantities and invoice against the applicable AMC rate card.
11. Payment Authorization
A financially valid invoice is authorized for payment.
12. RWA Verification
The RWA Representative confirms the repair or disputes/reopens it.
13. Closure
Confirmed work is closed. A defined no-response period may trigger automatic closure.
14. Rework Loop
A disputed ticket returns to the contractor for corrective action and repeats the completion/verification process.

14. Work Order Status Lifecycle
REPORTED → UNDER_REVIEW → VALIDATED → WORK_ORDER_CREATED → ASSIGNED → INSPECTION
→ ESTIMATE_SUBMITTED → PENDING_APPROVAL → APPROVED → IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED →
PENDING_VERIFICATION → VERIFIED → CLOSED
Exception path: PENDING_VERIFICATION → DISPUTED → REOPENED → IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED.

16. Scope Boundary
Included: infrastructure issue lifecycle, AMC-based pricing, contractor work orders, approvals, financial verification,
payment authorization, RWA verification, analytics, RBAC and auditability.
Not a core module: township-owned inventory/warehouse/resource-stock management. The contractor operates
under the AMC and bills according to the applicable contractual rates and actual approved work.
17. Overall System Chain
Township → Sector → Block → Infrastructure Asset → Complaint → Work Order → AMC Estimate →
Approval → Execution → Invoice → Finance Verification → RWA Verification → Closure

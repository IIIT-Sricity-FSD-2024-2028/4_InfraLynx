TIMS
Township Infrastructure Management System
Actors, Responsibilities, and End-to-End Workflow
Purpose: Describe each TIMS actor, their responsibilities, and the complete flow from complaint submission through repair, verification, invoice audit, and payment.
1. System Overview
TIMS is a township-focused infrastructure maintenance platform. Residents report infrastructure problems; the Desk Clerk validates and assigns work; contractors inspect and estimate the required work; authorized staff approve estimates; contractors complete repairs; the RWA Representative verifies the result; and Finance audits the invoice and handles payment. The Township COO oversees departments, township-wide work orders, and estimates that require executive approval.
TIMS is intended to support multiple townships, with access restricted to the user's township.
Department-specific users should be restricted to their department unless their role explicitly permits broader visibility.
Contractor estimates use official AMC (Annual Maintenance Contract) rate-card rates; the backend calculates totals and stores rate snapshots.
Inventory/stock management is out of scope for the current version. GPS features are deferred.
Work-order status and invoice/payment status must be tracked separately.
2. Actors and Responsibilities
1. RWA Representative
Submit infrastructure complaints with description, category, severity/priority, location details, and photos where available.
View permitted complaints and track status updates.
Verify completed work and confirm whether the repair is satisfactory.
Dispute incomplete or unsatisfactory work with a reason; disputed work can be reopened for rework.
Verification is a required gate before Finance can authorize payment.
2. Desk Clerk
Review new complaints in the department's triage queue.
Check complaint details, category, severity, photos, and possible duplicates.
Link confirmed duplicates to a master complaint; reject invalid complaints or reroute misclassified complaints where appropriate.
Validate complaints, create Work Orders, and assign eligible contractors.
Check contractor eligibility based on active status, township authorization, work category, and valid AMC coverage.
Monitor assignment and SLA deadlines; escalate delayed cases.
Does not perform the contractor's technical site inspection or normally approve contractor estimates.
3. Field Contractor
View assigned Work Orders in the Inspection List.
Inspect the site and submit findings, required work/quantities, and before-work evidence/photos.
Prepare an estimate using the official applicable AMC rate card; the backend calculates totals.
Respond to revision requests and resubmit estimates when needed.
Start work only after all required estimate approvals are complete.
Carry out repairs, record actual work/quantities, upload after-work photos, and submit completion details.
Perform rework if an RWA dispute reopens the Work Order.
4. Department Head
Manage employees in their department, including creating Desk Clerk accounts and updating, activating, or deactivating them.
Review estimates that require Department Head approval.
Approve, reject, or request revisions with reasons; forward estimates to the COO when required by configured rules.
Monitor departmental Work Orders, progress, SLA breaches, completion evidence, and RWA verification/disputes.
Escalate delayed or problematic Work Orders and review departmental dashboard metrics.
New Desk Clerk accounts inherit the Department Head's department and township; the assigned role is DESK_CLERK.
5. Finance Clerk / Finance Team
Register/manage contractors and their status, according to project permissions.
Create, maintain, renew, and track AMC contracts, validity dates, authorized work categories, and rate cards.
Audit invoices against the approved estimate, actual completed work, official AMC rates, and invoice line items.
Flag cost variances and place invoices on hold when required.
Authorize payment only after RWA verification is confirmed and the financial audit passes.
Record disbursement details, payment references/UTR, vouchers, and payment history.
May audit an invoice while RWA verification is pending, but cannot authorize payment until verification is complete.
6. Township COO
Oversee township-wide operations and department-level performance.
Create/update/activate/deactivate departments and assign or change Department Heads.
View Work Orders across departments, including status, contractor, estimate, progress, SLA, verification, and payment status as permitted.
Review and decide on estimates escalated for COO approval under configurable rules.
Review or escalate delayed work and inspect audit/activity records.
Does not replace contractor technical inspection, RWA quality verification, or Finance payment authorization.
7. TIMS Platform Owner / SaaS Administrator (outside township workflow)
Operates the TIMS software as a multi-tenant SaaS platform, including platform maintenance and subscription administration if implemented.
Is not a normal township operational actor and should not be confused with the township COO.
Platform-level subscription capabilities are outside the core complaint-to-payment workflow.
3. End-to-End Workflow
Step 1 — Complaint submission (RWA Representative)
The RWA Representative submits a complaint with description, category, priority/severity, location details, and photos where available. The system records it and applies the relevant SLA.
Step 2 — Triage and duplicate review (Desk Clerk)
The complaint enters the appropriate department's triage queue. The Desk Clerk reviews details and checks for duplicates. A confirmed duplicate is linked to the master complaint. Invalid complaints can be rejected and misclassified complaints can be rerouted.
Step 3 — Validate and create Work Order (Desk Clerk)
Once validated, the Desk Clerk creates a Work Order and assigns an eligible contractor. Eligibility should check active status, township authorization, applicable work category, and valid AMC coverage.
Step 4 — Site inspection (Field Contractor)
The assigned Work Order appears in the contractor's Inspection List. The contractor inspects the site, records findings and required quantities, and uploads before-work photos/evidence.
Step 5 — Estimate preparation (Field Contractor)
The contractor prepares an estimate using official AMC rate-card items. The backend retrieves the rates, calculates quantity × rate, and stores a rate snapshot so future rate-card changes do not rewrite estimate history.
Step 6 — Estimate approval (Department Head and possibly COO)
Every contractor-submitted estimate is sent to the Department Head for approval, regardless of amount. The Department Head can approve, reject, or request revisions. There is no amount-based threshold or automatic approval in this workflow.
Step 7 — Start and execute work (Field Contractor)
After every required approval is complete, the Work Order becomes eligible to start. The contractor starts work, records progress, and carries out the repair.
Step 8 — Completion submission (Field Contractor)
The contractor submits completion details, actual quantities/work performed, remarks, and after-work photos. The Work Order moves to pending RWA verification.
Step 9 — Quality verification (RWA Representative)
The RWA Representative reviews completed work. If satisfactory, they verify it. If incomplete or unsatisfactory, they submit a dispute reason; the Work Order is reopened for contractor rework and later re-verification.
Step 10 — Invoice audit (Finance)
Finance checks the invoice against the approved estimate, actual work evidence/quantities, applicable AMC rates, and invoice line items. Variances may be flagged and the invoice placed on hold. Audit can occur while RWA verification is pending, but payment authorization cannot.
Step 11 — Payment authorization and disbursement (Finance)
Finance authorizes payment only when RWA verification is confirmed and all financial checks pass. The disbursement is then recorded with its payment reference and supporting voucher/details. Authorization and actual payment are separate events.
Step 12 — Closure and recordkeeping
Once required verification and closure conditions are met, the Work Order is closed. The system preserves complaint, work-order, estimate, approval, verification, invoice, payment, and audit histories.

These are suggested states. The implementation should agree on one set of names and validate every transition in the backend.
Record type
Suggested statuses
Complaint
REPORTED → UNDER_REVIEW → VALIDATED (or REJECTED / REROUTED / LINKED_AS_DUPLICATE)
Work Order before execution
WORK_ORDER_CREATED → ASSIGNED → INSPECTION → ESTIMATE_SUBMITTED → PENDING_DEPT_HEAD_APPROVAL → APPROVED

IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED → PENDING_VERIFICATION → VERIFIED → CLOSED

Invoice
PENDING_AUDIT → ON_HOLD_DISPUTE / ON_HOLD_VARIANCE / APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT
Payment
AUTHORIZED → PAID (record actual disbursement separately)
6. Key Business Rules
Tenant isolation: operational queries must be scoped to the user's township; department users must also be scoped to their department where applicable.
Contractor eligibility: assign only active contractors authorized for the township and work category, with valid applicable AMC coverage.
AMC rate integrity: backend fetches official rates, calculates totals, and stores rate snapshots; do not trust client-submitted rate values.
Approval gates: contractors cannot start work until all approvals required by configured rules are complete.
Role separation: Desk Clerks handle triage/assignment; contractors inspect and estimate; Department Heads/COO approve according to rules; RWA verifies quality; Finance audits and authorizes payment.
Payment gate: Finance cannot authorize payment while RWA verification is pending/disputed or required financial checks have failed.
Separate statuses: Work Order progress, invoice audit, payment authorization, and actual disbursement must not be collapsed into one status.
Auditability: preserve who performed each significant action, when it occurred, and any reason/remarks.
No inventory/stock management is included in the current scope; GPS features are deferred.
7. Decisions to Confirm Before Implementation
Confirm whether any separate COO approval is required in addition to mandatory Department Head approval; no amount-based threshold is used.
Exact SLA rules and deadlines for each complaint category and priority.
Duplicate-detection distance and duplicate-linking policy.

Exact invoice and payment statuses and who can transition each status.
This document is a functional overview for planning and implementation. It does not prescribe a final database schema or replace the team's approval of business rules.
